Manifesto

Theater for the new millennium

Our goal is cultural change. Theater is stagnant, and we want to shape the next wave. We have two questions:

  1. What do people actually want?
  2. What are clever ways theater can scale?

And three potential insights.

Religion

Religion scales. Why? Because it knows how to create meaning, and it knows how to impress. The most reliable form of mass-market theater in America is the megachurch, the only place you can gibber and moan with thousands of your mortal kin under the influence of the Holy Ghost Himself.

This is peak theater. Not because religion is fake, but because it understands how to marry spectacle to the prime mover of the human soul—the deep, meaningful, transformative desire to connect to something real.

We fund projects that blur the line between spirituality and theater; projects that use ritual, psychotechnology, and church-like formats to create new mythic realities, nourish community, and provide hope in times of darkness. We are especially interested in projects that inspire audiences to attend on a regular basis (like a weekly service).

This type of theater scales through community and meaning-making.

Decentralization

This one is simple: people love being on their computers. They also like participating in weird collective internet experiments. We want to see if theater can scale over distributed mediums like Twitch and OnlyFans in a way that feels natural to the platform, without feeling lame or forced.

This type of theater scales in the obvious way, through the internet.

Gamification and Genre

In immersive theater, audiences want real agency and real stakes. Sleep No More was brilliant, but it kept the audience passive; you weren’t allowed to interact with the world or influence the plot. The real promise of immersive theater—and one that the medium has so far failed to consummate—is total immersion in an alternate reality that audiences can manipulate like an open-world video game.

The same people who want total immersion also love genre. They love genre fiction, genre movies, genre video games. They devour sci-fi and horror movies; they have an endless appetite for magic and an alarming need for romantasy. Except for the musical—and horror experiences in LA—theater has been slow to embrace genre because it still sees itself as high-brow.

But recent experiments are drawing large genre-loving crowds that don’t usually see theater, like Paranormal Activity (Broadway stage horror) and Twenty-Sided Tavern (interactive D&D theater).

What would immersive theater look like if it felt like you were inside a grimdark fantasy world? Or a video game like Disco Elysium? The world is still waiting for the Sleep No More of “video game theater,” and we want to fund it.

This type of theater scales through replay value and fandom.

A tunnel between the two coasts

The Bay Area is well-capitalized. It is the aggregate sum of the brightest minds on earth still hungry for truth and divine revelation. But it also has a severe culture deficit and a narrow understanding of value.

NYC has artists, culture, a renegade punk spirit and a high concentration of cool, talented, and unreasonably pretty people. But it is stagnant. Artists lack money. Funders are institutional dinosaurs with shockingly banal sensibilities; if they aren’t woke, they cling to formulas that barely work anymore.

The coasts need each other. What SF lacks in taste, it makes up for in its pursuit of the truly weird, truly outlier new thing. That makes it, oddly enough, the perfect audience for artists who want to take risks and remake the rules of their field.

The Bay also has esoteric knowledge about just how much the world is changing, and how fast, which artists would do well to recognize for their work to stay relevant.

We want to introduce experiential theater savants to Bay Area patrons interested in:

  1. Cultural change and scene-making
  2. Taste and artistic influence
  3. Unusual private experiences for them and their friends

Hopefully this can address the culture deficit!?

Tech is the most important business in the world, but a lot of people hate living in SF.

We think it’s because they don’t know how to spend their money. They invest in companies, not culture. Over time this creates a spiritual wasteland that afflicts even the most hardened autistic genius with dark waves of malaise in the night.

But art, especially when it is genuinely innovative and provocative, creates vibrant community, and community is the most powerful incubator for everything we love: inspiration, friendship, romance… even… even business.

Wealth has value not just when it creates more wealth, but when it transforms the ordinary world into a place of heightened enchantment. When SURPLUS meets TASTE, you get things like the Gilded Age and the Medici, who beautify our surroundings and inspire people to lead more interesting, well-rounded, and mythically resonant lives.

Otherwise the world is just a wire mother, giving milk but withholding all that is good and pure and true.

Absolution for purveyors of slop!

If you work for a company that thinks humanity could be annihilated by the merciless logic of superhuman evolution in the next few years: thank you for your service! We want to live fast and die young, but glory is capital-intensive and we need your glorious money to make glorious art before the world enters into permanent hyperflux. We will formally pardon you for unleashing the Abomination if you help us create MEANING FOR THE PEOPLE.

Plus, if all goes well we’ll soon be cattle grazing in the playgrounds of our artificial spawn, and the weird art experiments we inflict upon each other will be all we have left. Invest early in the Right-Brained Way, for the era of the Left is over! There is no better time to be a hysterical aesthete, mired to the hilt in unholy unprofitable things!
